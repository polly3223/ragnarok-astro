## Answers Versus Outcomes

A chatbot has done its job once it replies. An agent is judged on whether the work actually happened: the report filed, the ticket closed, the record updated. Moving from producing text to producing outcomes changes how the system is designed and trusted.

## Planning Before Acting

Before an agent touches anything, it turns a request into an ordered plan with inputs, checkpoints, and a clear definition of done. A chatbot improvises one reply at a time, which suits questions but struggles once a task spans several tools.

## Tools, Permissions, and Limits

Agents become useful through the tools they can call and safe through the limits placed on them. Scoped credentials, approvals for risky actions, and readable logs let a team hand over real responsibility without handing over everything.

## Choosing the Right Fit

Use a chatbot when people want quick answers and stay involved in every step. Reach for an agent when the same multi-step routine repeats each week and the value comes from finishing it reliably rather than talking about it.
